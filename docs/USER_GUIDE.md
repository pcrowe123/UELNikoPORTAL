# UEL / Niko Portal — how to use it

The portal is at **https://uelnikoportal.com**. It is one page with a list of our applications on
it. You sign in once to the portal, then click whichever application you want.

## Signing in

Go to the address above and put in your email and password.

Your portal account is made for you — there is no "sign up" button. If you have not got one, ask
Patrick. When he makes it you get an email with a link; that link is where you choose your own
password. Nobody else ever knows it, including Patrick.

If you have forgotten your password, click **I have forgotten my password** on the sign-in page and
put in your email. A link to set a new one arrives by email.

### The password is for the portal only

This is the one thing worth understanding about how the portal works.

Signing in to the portal gets you the **list** of applications. It does not sign you in to any of
them. When you click through to Booking, or Stock, or IQ, that application will ask you to sign in
to it, with the account you already have there.

So:

- Your portal password and your Booking password are different things. Please keep them different.
- Signing out of the portal does not sign you out of anything you opened from it.
- A tile you have no account for will let you through to that application and then turn you away at
  its own sign-in page. That is normal, and it is that application's administrator who can give you
  access.

## The landing page

Each application is a tile with its name, a line saying what it is for, and its web address.

Click one and it opens **in a new tab**, so the portal stays where it is behind it. If there are
more than four tiles there is a search box at the top right — type a few letters of a name, or of
what the thing does ("warehouse", "orders"), and the list narrows.

Because the tiles are ordinary links, everything you would expect from a link works: middle-click to
open in a background tab, ctrl-click the same, right-click to copy the address.

It all works on a phone, one tile per row.

## Putting the portal on your phone or desktop

There is an **Install** button at the top right. It puts the portal among your other apps — on a
phone's home screen, or on a Windows task bar or Mac dock — so it is one tap rather than a bookmark
you have to go looking for. It opens without the browser's address bar and tabs, which makes it feel
like an app rather than a web page.

On an **iPhone or iPad** the button cannot do it for you: Apple does not let a website install
itself. Tapping it shows you the three steps instead — **Share** (the square with an arrow), then
**Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**. It has to be Safari; Chrome and Firefox on an iPhone cannot
add to the home screen.

If you do not see the button at all, it is one of two things: the portal is already installed, or the
browser you are using cannot install it. The button hides rather than sitting there greyed out.

Installing changes nothing about how you sign in, and it does not store anything about the
applications on your device. It is a smarter shortcut, nothing more.

### A tile that says it needs fixing

Now and again a tile may appear greyed out, saying it has no usable web address. That means somebody
mistyped the address when the tile was set up. It is not your browser and it is not your account —
tell an administrator and it is a minute's work to correct.

## For administrators

If you are an administrator you have an **Admin** link in the top bar, with four tabs.

**Applications** is the tile list. **Add an application** asks for a name, a line about it and a web
address; you can leave off the `https://` and it will be added for you. Only secure (`https`)
addresses are accepted. The **Order** number decides where the tile sits on the landing page, lowest
first, and the colour is the little badge on the tile.

- **Hide** takes a tile off the landing page without losing it. Useful for an application that is
  down for the day.
- **Remove** takes it away for good. If anyone has ever opened that tile, it is retired rather than
  deleted, so the Activity list still makes sense.

Adding an application is just this — there is no release and no developer needed.

**People** shows everyone with a portal account. You can change somebody between Administrator and
Member, and deactivate somebody who has left, which stops them signing in to the portal at once.
You cannot change your own role or deactivate yourself — that is deliberate, so nobody can lock
everybody out, themselves included.

To add a new person, Patrick runs one command on the office PC. The tab shows you what it is.

Note that deactivating somebody here only closes the **portal** door. Their accounts on the
applications themselves are separate, and have to be closed in each application.

**Settings** holds the line of text under the heading on the landing page, whether tiles open in a
new tab, and whether the search box appears.

**Activity** shows the last 200 times somebody opened an application: who, which one, and when, in
Irish time. It is a courtesy rather than a guarantee — opening a tile with the middle mouse button
or from a copied address may not be counted.
